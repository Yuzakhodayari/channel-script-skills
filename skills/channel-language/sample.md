# Sample: original piece, blended mode (default dials)

Topic: the transformer shortage and your electricity bill. Not taken from any source transcript. Facts are rounded on purpose and should be re-checked before publishing. The usual figures are: large power transformers wait two to four years for delivery, roughly 80% of US units are imported (DOE, 2022), and prices have risen sharply since 2020.

---

So there's this grey metal box at the end of your street. You've walked past it a thousand times. You've never thought about it once. And right now, that box is one of the hardest things on Earth to buy.

I'm not exaggerating. If a utility orders one of the big ones today, the really big transformers that step power down from the grid, it's waiting two, three, sometimes four years. Four years. For a box.

But why? Because these things are basically hand-built. Copper wound by people, special steel that only a few mills make, and in the US most of them aren't even made at home. Roughly eight in ten get imported. So when demand spikes, there's no factory you can just flip on.

And demand is spiking. Data centers, EVs, heat pumps, new factories, all of it wants more power, all at the same time. And every new connection needs, you guessed it, a transformer.

Now here's the part nobody tells you. Who pays for the wait? You do. Prices for this gear have jumped a lot since 2020, and utilities don't eat that cost. It goes into the rate case, and the rate case goes into your bill.

So the next time your bill goes up and someone blames "the market", just remember the grey box. It's not that complicated. We built an economy that runs on electricity, and then we forgot to build the boxes that deliver it.

Will it get fixed? Probably. Eventually. New plants are being announced. But they take years too. Which means, for a while at least, the box wins.
