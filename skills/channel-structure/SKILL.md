# Channel structure (strategy and architecture)

## Purpose
Give a video the skeleton these channels use: a cold open on an event or number, a promise line within about 6% of runtime, about 7 major turns spread from 10% to 75%, a bridged sponsor read near 35-42% (Gen/mechanism modes only), a counter-case pass, a conclusion pivot at about 82-88%, and a 1-3 line sign-off. Every number here is **measured** on host-only transcripts of 31 videos and weighted per video: Adam 7, Gen 4, Oil 5, Especially 3, After 1. The weighting lands at roughly Adam 35% / Gen 40% / Esp 9% / Oil 5% / After 12% of total influence.

## When to use
- "Outline / beat out / structure a video on …"
- "How long should this be?", "where does the sponsor go?", "when do I state the thesis?"
- "Make chapters", "the middle drags", "the ending feels rushed"
- Reviewing a draft's architecture before writing sentences
- Choosing between **Adam mode** (long investigative narrative), **Gen mode** (finance/econ explainer), **Mechanism mode** (oil/SpaceX/data-center: how-it-works with high stakes), and **Others** (general explainer)

## Pick the mode first (measured; weighted medians)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Hook (cold open → promise) | ≈64 s / 186 words / 2.5% | ≈85 s / 266 words / 6% | ≈95 s / 6% | ≈88 s | 79 s / 225 words / 6% |
| Major turns | 7 | 6 | 7 | 5-7 | 7 |
| Sponsor | none mid-video | ≈38.6% (IQR 34.5-41.7%), ≈45 s, bridged | top-flagged (4-6%) or ≈27% | varies | 18/31 have one |
| Conclusion starts | ≈76-82% | ≈83-86% | ≈90-95% | ≈86% | 87.6% |
| Sign-off | 1 line at 99.9% | 1-3 lines | 1-3 lines | 1-3 lines | last 1-3% |

Topic → mode: a story with named people and dated events → Adam. Money, prices, or an industry squeeze aimed at a viewer's wallet → Gen. A physical or industrial system with planetary stakes → Mechanism.

## Workflow
1. **Facts ledger.** List dated events, named actors, numbers with sources, and documents. Any beat that needs a missing fact gets `[NEED SOURCE]`. Never invent.
2. **Mode + runtime.** Pick from the table. Set a target word count = runtime × WPM.
3. **Title-script contract.** Write the title's promise in one sentence. The hook must name the title's key noun or claim inside 30 s (S title_in_hook).
4. **Cold open (0 → hook end).** Choose a type: dated moment ("For 16 hours on Tuesday, July 8th…"), origin-with-a-twist ("In the year 2000, a 21-year-old…"), absurd number, viewer-assumption flip, or "what no one is talking about". No background in the first two sentences.
5. **Scope + thesis.** Narrow the claim ("not the whole X — the part that…") and state the stakes for the viewer. Where detected, this lands at about 5% (S thesis).
6. **Promise / roadmap line** at hook end: "I want to take you through…", "to understand this, the best way is…", "let's break down…". Adam ≈1:04, Gen ≈1:24.
7. **Act plan with turns.** Place about 7 turns (6 in Gen mode) using the template positions in `references/beat-maps.md`. Turns are questions or "Now, / But then / So now let's talk about…" pivots, not topic labels. Turn density peaks at 20-30% and 50-60%.
8. **Escalation ladder.** Order sections from person → company → system → the viewer. The skeptic or counter-case pass goes at about 55-70%.
9. **Sponsor (if any).** Gen mode: after the problem is fully stated (35-42%), with a bridge ("that's exactly where today's sponsor … comes in"), 35-60 s, then a re-entry as a question or loop ("So back to it, because if …?"). Mechanism mode may flag the sponsor at the top instead. Adam mode: none mid-video.
10. **Open-loop ledger.** Every promise in the hook ("details I haven't seen reported anywhere else") gets a row with the section that pays it.
11. **Conclusion pivot** at the mode's position: a meaning question ("So what does all this mean?", "which brings up … what this all means for you"), synthesis, a callback to the cold open, and agency for the viewer.
12. **Sign-off.** 1-3 lines in the last 1-3%. Adam: "Thank you so much for watching and see you in the next one."
13. **Chapters.** One per major turn, phrased as the question or claim of that section.
14. **Self-check** with the rubric below, then hand off to channel-storytelling, channel-audience, and channel-language.

## Core rules (memorise)
1. Open on an event, a number, or a scene. Never on history or a definition.
2. Promise by ~6% (Adam ~2.5%). The viewer knows the contract before 1:30.
3. About 7 turns. No 5-minute stretch without one.
4. Turns are questions or pivots that move the story.
5. A sponsor gets a bridge in and a question out, 35-60 s, at 35-42% in Gen mode.
6. Run the counter-case before the conclusion.
7. The conclusion starts at 76-88% depending on mode, and turns toward "you" and the future.
8. Close in 1-3 lines. One thank-you, one handoff.
9. Title words in the hook. Pay off the title's promise before 50%, and again in the close.
10. Pick one mode per video. Don't mix narrative scenes with lecture blocks inside an act.

## Lessons from the exams (folded every 10; see `exams/exam-log.md`)
- **CTA lines are not just "subscribe".** Corpus sign-offs are "Thank you so much for watching", "I'll see you in the next one", "comment…". Label any line at 97-100% with thanks or see-you as CTA.
- **Promise lines have more forms than "I want to take you…"**: "So to understand this whole thing…", "But what no one is talking about is…", "the full story". Several promise misses were a "But what no one is talking about…" line misread as a section turn because of its "But" opener. Position (≤8%) beats opener words.
- **Section turns open with So / Now / But / Because / We.** The "Because" and "We" openers were learned from misses.
- **Conclusions often open with "But here's the thing."**, "I think…", or "So what does all this mean?", not with "in conclusion".
- **Sponsor reads start at the bridge**, ("that's where/why … today's sponsor"). Gen/7's unpunctuated captions put its sponsor at 20%, an outlier.
- **Gen outlines drifted.** Gold hook ends ran later than the 6% prior (≈8-10% in several Gen videos), and conclusions ran earlier (≈78%). Use 6-9% for the Gen hook and 78-86% for the Gen conclusion.
- **Openers are idiosyncratic.** Learned weights on "number" and "named person" swung both ways. The stable signals were: no anaphora (doesn't start with this/that/he/also), no "remember / at this point / by the way" meta, and self-contained. Opener MCQs were the weakest exam type (0.53). Don't trust a single feature.
- **Mechanism vs Gen vs Others profiles overlap.** Runtime separates Adam cleanly. The others need "you" density plus hook length.

## Self-check rubric (score each 0-2; ship at ≥ 16/20)
1. Cold open is an event, number, or scene with no background (S001-S010).
2. Promise or roadmap line is present by the mode's position.
3. Thesis or stakes for the viewer is stated in the hook.
4. Turn count and spread match the mode (±2; no gap > 5 min).
5. Each turn is a question or pivot, and chapters are named from it.
6. Escalation goes person → system → you. Counter-case present.
7. Sponsor is bridged, sized, and placed per mode, with a question re-entry (or none, in Adam mode).
8. Every open loop in the ledger is paid.
9. Conclusion pivot sits at the mode's position, with a meaning question, callback, and agency.
10. Sign-off is ≤3 lines, and the title promise is honoured in the hook and close.

## Hard limits
- Never invent quotes, events, or numbers. Use `[NEED SOURCE]`.
- Do not copy a corpus video's beat map wholesale for a new topic. Use the templates.
- This skill does not change the adam-voice, gen-voice, or script-voice skills. Use them for sentence voice.

## v2 checklists

**Beat position table** (median % of runtime, TRAIN videos): PROMISE/ROADMAP 6, SPONSOR 42, SECTION TURN 50, CONCLUSION 78, CTA 99.
- [ ] **Unmarked hook-end rule:** a line with no beat cue and no question closes the cold open, at about 6%. Never park uncued lines at 50%.
- [ ] A mid-video CTA (no "thanks for watching", "see you" or "bye") is still placed late. These creators rarely CTA mid-video, except through a sponsor bridge.
- [ ] A sponsor bridge ("that's exactly why…", "speaking of…") is placed between 15% and 90% and is labelled SPONSOR.
- [ ] Questions of 6 or more words mid-video are SECTION TURN re-hooks.

**Opener check (T3).**
- [ ] A real first sentence is shorter, often uses "you" or "I", and is not reflective or promise-like.
- [ ] Openers in the blend are of one kind: dated event, viewer instruction, number fact, invitation ("So, let's…") or sweeping claim.

**Reorder check (T6).** Map each beat to its table position and sort. Ties go in this order: promise, then turn, then conclusion, then CTA.
