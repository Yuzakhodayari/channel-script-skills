## Opening (~400 words)
Imagine you open your mailbox and find a letter from your insurance company. It's polite. It thanks you for being a loyal customer. And then, in the second paragraph, it tells you that next year they won't be covering your house anymore. Not because you filed a claim. Not because you did anything wrong. They're just leaving.

Now, you'd probably think that's a mistake. You'd call them. And they'd tell you the same thing they're telling hundreds of thousands of other people: it's not personal, it's the math.

So here's the question. If the people whose entire job is measuring risk have decided your home isn't worth insuring, what do they know that you don't? And what happens to a neighborhood, a city, or a whole state when the safety net that sits under every mortgage just quietly walks away?

In this video I'm going to show you exactly who is pulling out, why the numbers stopped working, and the one part of this story almost nobody is talking about: the insurers of the insurers. Because that's where this really starts.

And it's not just one state. [SOURCE: count of states with major carrier exits.] It's not just hurricanes either. It's wildfire, it's hail, it's flooding, and it's the cost of rebuilding a house that's gone up faster than almost anything else you can buy.

But before we get into that, if you've ever looked at your own policy and had no idea what you were actually paying for, that's exactly the kind of thing today's sponsor helps with...

[SPONSOR READ — 60-75 s]

So, back to that letter. To understand why it shows up, you first need to understand one strange fact about insurance companies: they don't really want your money. They want your *predictable* money. And predictability is the one thing that's disappearing.

Here's how it works.
